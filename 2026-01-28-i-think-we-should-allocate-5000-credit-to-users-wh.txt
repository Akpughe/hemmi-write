⏺ This is a solid freemium strategy. A few thoughts:

  Why it works:
  - Removes friction at signup - users can immediately experience value
  - "Show don't tell" - research results speak for themselves
  - Creates a natural conversion moment when credits run out and they're already
   invested in writing

  Things to consider:

  1. Is 5000 credits calibrated correctly? It should be enough for 1-2
  meaningful research sessions to demonstrate value, but not so generous that
  users never need to pay. You'd want to check how many credits a typical deep
  research query consumes.
  2. Abuse prevention - Multiple account creation to farm free credits is a
  common problem. Consider:
    - Email verification
    - Phone verification for credit grant
    - IP/device fingerprinting
    - Delayed credit grant (e.g., after first action)
  3. Clear UX around credit balance - Users should always see their remaining
  credits and understand when they'll need to pay. Surprise paywalls feel bad;
  anticipated ones feel fair.
  4. The transition moment matters - When credits run out mid-workflow (e.g.,
  they've done research but haven't written yet), that's high-intent. The
  payment prompt at that moment should be frictionless.

  Overall, I think this is a reasonable approach - letting users prove value to
  themselves before asking for money tends to work better than gates upfront,
  especially for a tool where the output quality is the main selling point.
